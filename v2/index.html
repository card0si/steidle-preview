Steidle Fabricating / Waterjet cutting

Waterjet cutting in Northern Kentucky

A waterjet cuts with a hair-thin stream of water and garnet abrasive at very high pressure. There is no flame and no heat, so the part comes off the table flat, with the same properties the material had going on.

Quote this jobCall 859-581-0122

Why cut with water

Plasma and laser cut by melting. That leaves a heat-affected zone along the edge: a band of metal that has been hardened, discolored or warped. On thin sheet it can pull the part out of flat. On tool steel or hardened plate it can make the edge too hard to drill or tap.

A waterjet erodes the material instead of melting it. The edge is the same metal as the middle of the plate. You can tap it, machine it or weld it without fighting a hardened skin, and parts stay flat even when they are long and narrow.

The stream is narrow, so parts nest close together and inside corners come out tight. There is no tooling to make, which is why a single part is as reasonable to cut as fifty.

What a waterjet cuts

Nearly anything that can lie flat on the table. Most of our work is metal, but the same machine cuts materials a laser or plasma can't touch.

  • Mild and carbon steel plate
  • Stainless steel
  • Aluminum, including thick plate
  • Brass, bronze and copper
  • Tool steel and hardened plate
  • Plastics, including UHMW and acrylic
  • Rubber and gasket material
  • Composites and laminates

One exception worth knowing: tempered glass shatters under any waterjet. Ask us about anything unusual before you buy material.

Common questions

Waterjet, laser or plasma: which do I need?

Use a waterjet when heat is a problem, when the material is thick, reflective or non-metal, or when the edge will be machined or tapped afterward. Laser is faster on thin sheet in high volume. Plasma is quick on rough structural cuts. If you aren't sure, describe the part and we'll tell you honestly.

How thick can you cut?

It depends on the material. Call 859-581-0122 with the material and thickness and we'll tell you whether it fits our table before you order anything.

I don't have a CAD file. Can you still cut my part?

Yes. Bring a dimensioned sketch, a paper template or the part you want copied, and we'll work out a cut file from it.

Will the edge need finishing?

A waterjet edge is clean and slightly matte, like a fine sandblast. Many parts go straight into service. Tell us if an edge needs to be a sealing surface or a precise fit and we'll plan for machining.

  • Quantity, and whether it repeats
  • Which edges or holes matter most

Welding and weld repair in Bromley, KY

New fabrication and repair welding from a family shop that has been joining metal in Northern Kentucky since 1955. Bring it in, or send the drawing and we'll build it from raw stock.

Quote this jobCall 859-581-0122

Fabrication welding

A weldment is only as good as its fit-up. When parts are cut out of square, the welder spends the day filling gaps and pulling things straight, and the heat from all that extra weld is what warps a frame.

We cut our own parts on the waterjet, so tabs land in slots and holes line up before anyone strikes an arc. That means less weld, less heat and less grinding, and a frame that sits flat on the floor when it's done.

Repair welding

Things crack, wear through and get backed into. A lot of what comes through the door is a broken part and a deadline. We'll look at how it failed before welding it back together, because a bracket that cracked once in the same spot usually needs a gusset, not just a bead.

If the part is past saving, we can cut and machine a new one from the old one as a pattern.

Where machining fits

The waterjet gets a part to shape. Machining gets it to size. A cut hole is fine for a clearance bolt, but a bearing bore, a threaded hole or a face that has to seal needs to be machined.

Because both happen here, the blank moves from the waterjet table to the machine without leaving the building. You get one part number, one quote and one shop responsible for whether it fits.

Replacement parts

Older equipment runs on parts nobody stocks anymore. If you have the worn or broken piece, bring it in. We can measure it, work out what it was supposed to be before it wore, and make a new one.

This is often quicker and cheaper than hunting a discontinued part number, and it gets a machine back to work.

Steidle Fabricating / Custom metal signs

Custom metal signs, cut in Northern Kentucky

Signs, letters and logos cut from plate on our waterjet. One sign for the end of the driveway or a set for every door in the building, made a few miles from where it will hang.

Made the way ours was

The sign in front of our shop is cut steel plate. Left raw, steel weathers to the deep brown you see here and holds that look for decades. It is the same material and the same cutting we'd put into yours.

A waterjet follows any line you can draw, so script lettering, fine logos and inside corners all come out crisp. Because it cuts cold, thin letters don't warp or discolor on the way off the table.

Signs we cut

  • Business signs and storefront logos
  • Address plaques and house numbers
  • Farm, ranch and property entrance signs
  • Family name and monogram signs
  • Memorial and dedication plaques
  • Garage, bar and workshop signs
  • Team, school and church logos
  • Individual metal letters and numbers
  • Trail, park and wayfinding markers
  • Wedding, anniversary and retirement gifts

Material and finish

Steel is the classic. Leave it raw to rust to a patina, or have it painted or coated for color. Stainless stays bright and suits kitchens, breweries and modern storefronts. Aluminum is light, won't rust and is easy to hang on a wall.

Tell us where the sign will live, indoors or out, and how you plan to mount it. We can cut mounting holes, tabs for stakes or slots for standoffs into the sign itself so it goes up clean.

Common questions

What kind of artwork do you need?

A vector file (AI, EPS, SVG, DXF or a vector PDF) is ideal. A clean black and white image or a photo of a sketch works too. We'll let you know if anything in the design is too fine to cut at the size you want.

How big or small can a sign be?

Tell us the size you're picturing. Very thin strokes and tiny inside details have limits that depend on material thickness, and we'll flag those before cutting.

Can you cut my business logo exactly?

Yes. A waterjet follows the outline of your artwork, so the proportions stay true. Letters with enclosed centers, like O and A, need small bridges or a backing plate, and we'll show you the options.

Do you make one sign, or only batches?

One is fine. So is a set of matching signs for a whole property.
